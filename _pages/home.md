**Why do people respond differently to the same diet or lifestyle behavior, and what is the genetic and molecular basis for that variability?**

The Westerman Lab sits at the intersection of genetic epidemiology and precision cardiometabolic disease prevention. We develop and apply methods for gene-environment interaction (GxE) analysis at biobank scale, and use them to understand the heterogeneity in how individuals respond to diet, physical activity, adiposity, and other modifiable exposures.

Our work combines large-scale human genetics, multi-omics integration, and nutritional epidemiology, drawing on datasets including the UK Biobank, *All of Us*, TOPMed, and the Mass General Brigham Biobank. We build open-source software — including [GEM](https://github.com/large-scale-gxe-methods/GEM), the most widely used tool for genome-wide interaction testing — so that these analyses are tractable for the broader community.

## What we work on

* **[Large-scale genome-wide interaction studies](/research/#large-scale-genome-wide-interaction-studies)** — methods and discovery for GxE testing across the genome and the exposome.
* **[Polygenic scores for interaction and response](/research/#polygenic-scores-for-interaction-and-response)** — genetic scores designed to predict *response* to an intervention rather than *risk* of disease.
* **[Molecular mechanisms of GxE](/research/#molecular-mechanisms-of-gene-environment-interaction)** — using metabolomics and other omics to explain why an interaction exists.
* **[Response to omega-3 fatty acids](/research/#heterogeneity-in-response-to-omega-3-fatty-acids)** — genetic modifiers of cardiometabolic response to a widely used dietary supplement.
* **[Response to adiposity and weight loss](/research/#heterogeneity-in-response-to-adiposity-and-weight-loss)** — who benefits most, metabolically, from reducing adiposity.

## Where we are

The lab is based in the [Clinical and Translational Epidemiology Unit](https://www.massgeneral.org/medicine/cteu) at Massachusetts General Hospital, with an additional presence at the [Broad Institute](https://www.broadinstitute.org/) (Diabetes Initiative and Diabetes Genetics Research Group) and the [MGH Center for Genomic Medicine](https://www.massgeneral.org/genomic-medicine).
